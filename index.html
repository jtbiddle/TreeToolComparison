LeafCut, Foliage Toolkit and Masked Material to Nanite compared

LeafCut, Foliage Toolkit Plus and Masked Material to Nanite each replace alpha-masked foliage cards with geometry cut to the leaf shape, so the material can be opaque and Nanite no longer evaluates opacity over the transparent card area. All three were run on the same seven Fab meshes: two grasses, a bush, ivy and three trees.

Masked Material to Nanite needs a mask texture and threshold for each material, and Foliage Toolkit's threshold on the two grasses was set the same way; where a material squares or scales alpha before clipping, the threshold is the equivalent texture value. The only quality setting changed from default was Foliage Toolkit's Simplify on the ivy (2 instead of 8). No settings sweep was run, so triangle counts and outline scores describe these settings, not each plugin's best result.

Test meshes

Four small plants, three trees. The set includes isolated leaves, dense canopies, hanging ivy, mixed bark and leaf cards, and view-dependent opacity. Select a mesh to open its matched image comparison.

Single-card geometry

One source card per plant, unfolded onto its texture, with the triangles each plugin saved for that card. Overlap, extra and lost area are texel measurements on that card only.

Opaque conversion

An output counts only if every material on it is opaque; a masked material left on the output keeps the masked Nanite cost there. The limits below come from plugin source and did not change with any setting we found.

LeafCut

  • Evaluates each material's opacity-mask graph rather than reading a single texture.
  • Opacity that changes with viewing angle is fixed at one angle by the cut.
  • No hard limit was hit on the seven test meshes.

Foliage Toolkit

  • Fixed limit of 8 million texels per card region. A card spread over a large tiling texture, such as bark, stays masked.
  • Tree output (Plus) keeps the source's masked materials. We found no setting that makes them opaque.
  • Reads the mask texture directly. If the material changes alpha before clipping, you match the threshold by hand.

Masked Material to Nanite

  • Takes one mask texture, channel and threshold per material and does not evaluate the material graph. Alpha that is squared or scaled before clipping maps to an equivalent threshold; opacity that varies with viewing angle cannot be represented.
  • Fixed limit of 268 million texels of UV area per mesh, which large tiling bark can exceed.
  • Static meshes only.

    Controls

    LeafCut derives its inputs from the material. Foliage Toolkit exposes the most settings and saves them per tree. Masked Material to Nanite requires the mask inputs per material.

    How each plugin handles each part of the job

    Triangles versus accuracy

    Triangle count and outline fit trade against each other, and each plugin's settings sit at a different point. On the four small meshes, LeafCut and Masked Material to Nanite matched the outline about equally, with Masked Material to Nanite using two to five times the triangles. Foliage Toolkit used the fewest triangles and matched the outline less closely. Trees are excluded because the plugins count assembly triangles differently.

    Timings and stages

    Summary

    LeafCut produced opaque output for all seven meshes, trees included, without per-material setup. It has no preview, no per-material settings and no Blueprint or Python API.

    Foliage Toolkit has the most controls. Its tree output kept masked leaf materials on all three trees, and the bush output kept a masked bark card. The small-plant cutter is also available free as Lite.

    Masked Material to Nanite cut closely on the four small meshes at the highest triangle counts. It outputs static meshes only and needs a mask texture and threshold assigned per material; a fixed texture threshold cannot represent the view-angle fade on the oak and maple leaves.

    Whole-plant silhouette

    The card comparison measures the cut itself. This score checks the assembled plant's visible shape, including overlap between cards. Each source and output was rendered from the same fixed cameras. Overlap is shared silhouette pixels divided by all pixels covered by either; lost and extra are removed and added pixels as percentages of the source's visible area.

    GPU time

    Lower is faster. Whole-frame GPU time includes sky, ground, shadows and post-processing in every bar. The original masked cards with Nanite switched on are the baseline; converted results receive a timing only when they are fully opaque.

    Exclusive Graphics-queue timer scopes from the same frames, grouped. Main-view Nanite is Nanite visibility-buffer rasterization and its base pass for the camera view.     Wind cost

    Each tree output was timed in a field of trees of one species, with Unreal's DynamicWind on and then off, on the same placements and camera path. LeafCut and Foliage Toolkit build skeletal trees; Masked Material to Nanite outputs static meshes and is not included. Each change is that output's own wind overhead. Toolkit's trees keep masked leaf materials, so its times include masked rendering and are not a like-for-like comparison with LeafCut.

    Method

    Inputs

    • Every cut starts from the same imported LOD0, on an isolated copy. Where a plugin couldn't represent the source material, no cut was forced and the result is marked Incompatible.

    Outline scores

    • 2048 × 2048 subject-only captures, 40° field of view, wind and world-position offset off, bind pose.
    • Wildflowers: front at near, mid and far distances, plus side and oblique near. Trees: front, side and oblique near. Other plants: front near. Each plant uses the same cameras for every plugin.
    • A pixel counts as covered when opacity is at least 50%.
    • Outline only. Colour, shading, normals and hidden interior leaves are not scored.
